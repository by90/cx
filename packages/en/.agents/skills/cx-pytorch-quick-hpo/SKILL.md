# cx PyTorch Lightweight HPO

## Language Rules

- Use the package language for conversations, explanations, plans, summaries, review decisions, verification evidence, and cx documents. Do not mix languages inside prose fragments or term lists.
- In Chinese-package work, if an English identifier, command, path, API name, library, protocol, standard, proper name, or ambiguity-sensitive term must remain in English, explain its meaning, role, and local context in Chinese in the same sentence or an adjacent sentence. In English-package work, explain unavoidable non-English terms in English.

## Resource-Safety Gate

The first step of every lightweight-HPO experiment is to estimate expected system-memory and VRAM use, then decide whether the run may start. Record at least currently available system memory, currently available VRAM, expected peak use, the safety margin reserved for the operating system and Codex App, and the decision to start, adjust only unfixed dimensions, or wait. The estimate may use model structure, tensor shapes, batch size, and a minimal trial run, but must not start the full experiment merely to discover whether resources will be exhausted. One tenth of complete-entity samples is the lightweight-HPO data boundary and must not be reduced for resource reasons. A user-fixed window, batch, or model must not change for resource reasons; if unfixed dimensions still cannot be adjusted into a safe range, wait for sufficient resources.

Any task expected to consume substantial resources or run for a long time must start in an external terminal outside Codex App, so Codex App's built-in terminal does not host the training process or its high-volume output. Continuously write training progress, standard output, standard error, system memory, process memory, VRAM use, per-epoch metrics, and errors to file-based logs. The agent must analyze experiment status by reading those log files, without relying on scrolling terminal output, terminal control sequences, or other special terminal handling. This gate prevents resource exhaustion and system crashes, especially Codex App crashes caused by the training process or terminal-output load.

## Purpose

Quick HPO is the lightweight-HPO stage defined by this skill. Within user constraints, it carries most parameter selection on a smaller but complete sample. Features and parameters explicitly fixed by the user remain unchanged. Every other important dimension must enter the search or have an evidence-backed exclusion reason, including model structure and capacity, learning-rate ranges, optimizers, schedulers, and sample characteristics such as sliding-window length. It keeps 5 candidates and completes per-candidate ablation and backtesting before full-data tuning.

## Highest-Priority Objective

Quick HPO must keep 5 candidates that jointly approach all four of these objectives across comparable trials:

1. Lower best validation loss, `best_val_loss`, under the same label, validation set, loss definition, and validation cadence.
2. More effective `val_loss` improvements under the same effective-improvement threshold.
3. A completed-epoch count as close as possible to the fixed `120`-epoch cap; completing all `120` epochs is a continuous preference, not a single eligibility gate.
4. A best epoch as close as possible to epoch `120`, not merely close to the end of an early-stopped run.

All four objectives jointly have the highest priority in five-candidate selection, and no single objective may decide admission by itself. A candidate that is no better on any of the four objectives than another candidate must not rank ahead of it. When no candidate leads all four, use the 5 slots to preserve different tradeoffs and explain each one without inventing an unapproved numeric weighting formula. Test loss, `test_loss`, backtest results, training speed, and resource utilization only confirm generalization, confirm business behavior, or constrain execution; they must not replace the four joint objectives.

## Iron Rules

1. The training, inference, and backtest entrypoints must run by themselves with default configuration; menus and tuning orchestrators may only call those scripts or stable entrypoints.
2. A tuning run must initialize from the target model's default config, then let the shared tuning facility clone and mutate typed config objects in memory before passing them to training, inference, or backtest entrypoints. Every trial behavior change must affect only the in-memory config object, including fields, labels, window length, batch size, learning rate, optimizer, scheduler, model structure, output directory, and saved snapshots. Do not create temporary YAML files or temporary config directories as a trial fact source, do not rewrite current config files for trial behavior, and do not implement separate config-rewrite flows inside individual model directories.
3. When saving checkpoints or weights, persist the complete config snapshot associated with the in-memory recipe alongside that artifact. The tuning script may persist trial configs, best recipes, metrics, and backtest configs into the output directory, but must never rewrite the current project config, default config, or user-maintained config files.
4. Lightweight tuning uses exactly one tenth of all complete-entity samples, and the sampling unit must be a complete entity. For daily data from 2,000 stocks, select 200 stocks and keep each selected stock's full daily history. When the entity count is not divisible by `10`, record the rounding rule, actual entity count, and actual ratio.
5. Study every field actually used for business meaning, label relationship, distribution, and leakage risk. Run contribution experiments, noise analysis, and candidate comparisons only for tunable fields. User-fixed fields retain semantic and leakage evidence but must not enter field-combination search or ablation.
6. Field research for every used field, together with contribution evidence for tunable fields, must be documented in the target `docs/cx` scenario, task, change, or experiment-summary area.
7. Separate the semantic roles of every used daily base field, market-strength indicator, position indicator, and ranking indicator: daily fields are the base; market-strength indicators measure cross-sectional overall strength; position indicators measure location in a time window; ranking indicators measure cross-sectional comparison.
8. Understand ranking fields that are actually used and run comparative checks only when they are tunable. They usually correspond to base fields, so tunable comparisons determine whether using base and ranking fields together is harmful, whether ranking fields alone are sufficient, and whether ranking plus position fields works.
9. When field selection is tunable, give explicit recommendations for enabled fields and features. Use one fixed model to validate field combinations and document the recommendation. Skip this search when the user fixed the field selection.
10. Staged search only isolates variables and controls the experiment budget; it is not an importance order. Skip user-fixed dimensions without freezing any other unspecified dimension, and use the tuning-scope ledger required below as the source of those decisions.
11. When window length is tunable, search it together with batch size. Longer windows increase memory and compute cost, and attention-like or pairwise representations can grow close to quadratically with window length, so identify the safe batch set separately for every window. During candidate formation, search window lengths only in the closed interval from `3` through `20` days. After forming an initial set of `5` candidates, hold every other parameter fixed and test `30`, `40`, `50`, and `60` days for the candidate currently strongest on the four joint objectives. If the window changes, jointly rerun all five final complete recipes before confirming the final candidates with the four objectives. When the user fixed the window, skip both base-window search and long-window extension while searching other unfixed dimensions.
12. On high-memory hardware such as two NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPUs with 32 GB VRAM per card, VRAM use, GPU utilization, and training throughput only determine the safely runnable range and experiment budget. When batch size is unfixed, use `8192`, `4096`, `2048`, `1024`, and `512` as a descending exploration order for the safe batch set of each window and field recipe. Every safe candidate still needs a comparable trial and four-objective evaluation; a larger or faster batch must not advance for speed alone. When the user fixed batch size, preserve it; if that fixed value cannot run safely, wait for resources or ask the user to change the constraint.
13. Every lightweight candidate trial is planned for `120` epochs with a fixed cap of `120`, monitors `val_loss`, and uses default early-stopping patience `20`. When a learning-rate warmup and cooldown cycle is used, explicitly configure and save the scheduler name, warmup epochs, cooldown epochs, cycle count, maximum learning rate, minimum learning rate, change function, and actual patience. Adjust patience only when one complete cycle exceeds `20` epochs; prefer shortening the cycle or keeping patience at or below `30`, and record the reason and actual value when more than `30` is unavoidable.
14. Every trial must record the effective-improvement threshold, actual patience, whether it completed all `120` epochs, the effective improvement count in the final `20` epochs, the total effective `val_loss` improvement count and improvement ratio, the best epoch, the final epoch, the best epoch as a proportion of `120`, and the distance from the best epoch to epoch `120` and to the actual final epoch. Candidate comparison always uses one-based epoch numbers; a framework's zero-based epoch index must be incremented before computing distance to epoch `120`. Completing all `120` epochs and improving in the final `20` epochs are evidence for the four joint objectives, not standalone eligibility gates. The best-to-final distance is diagnostic only and must not replace the highest-priority objective.
15. For classification tasks, use `val_loss`/cross-entropy as the default training objective, tuning monitor, and early-stopping monitor. Record accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and AUC only as business evaluation metrics.
16. In multi-model projects, each model may have its own unique best field set, window length, label, and capacity configuration. Lightweight tuning must keep each model's per-model best candidate instead of prematurely keeping only one cross-model global ranking.
17. Lightweight tuning must establish a five-minute tracking cadence: take a baseline sample immediately after startup, then report every 5 minutes with the current trial, current epoch, latest `val_loss`, current `best_val_loss`, effective-improvement threshold, effective `val_loss` improvement count, elapsed trial time, average epoch time, estimated remaining trials or epochs, GPU utilization, VRAM usage, peak VRAM, system memory, and current process memory. If no epoch or artifact changes within a 5-minute window, report the stalled position, process id, and resource-log path being written.
18. Every epoch and every trial must be written to a shared resource-monitoring artifact. Epoch records must include the epoch index, one-based epoch number, start and end time, epoch duration, cumulative trial duration, training or validation `val_loss`, current `best_val_loss`, GPU utilization, allocated, reserved, peak, and free VRAM, total and available system memory, process memory, sample throughput, OOM status, and error reason. Trial-end records must include total trial duration, average epoch duration, completed epoch count, early-stop epoch, effective-improvement threshold, effective `val_loss` improvement count, `best_val_loss`, `final_val_loss`, `test_loss`, backtest metrics, and resource-log path.
19. Lightweight-HPO candidate reports must include Top10 business metrics: Top10 hit rate, mean gain for hit candidates, mean gain for missed candidates, Top10 downside share, and mean loss among downside candidates. The five-candidate comparison table must also include trial name, stage, key recipe, `best_val_loss`, `final_val_loss`, `test_loss`, completed epochs, effective `val_loss` improvement count, best epoch, distance from the best epoch to epoch `120` and to the actual final epoch, trial duration, average epoch duration, and those Top10 metrics.
20. Every random process in sampling, trial initialization, model initialization, data shuffling, ablation, and backtesting must read the seed config whose value is `3407` from the config subsystem. All candidates in the same comparison use that value; tuning scripts must not embed another seed constant or derive unrecorded per-candidate seeds.
21. Lightweight HPO keeps exactly `5` final candidates. After forming the initial five, complete the long-window extension in rule 11 when window length is tunable. If that extension changes a window, jointly rerun all five final complete recipes and reconfirm the final candidates with the four joint objectives; a long-window result must not update a candidate without that joint comparison. Then run ablation and backtesting for every final candidate. For ablation, hold that candidate's sample, seed, and training contract fixed while removing tunable selected data groups, model-structure modules, or key training components one at a time, then record the change and conclusion. Features and parameters fixed by the user must not be removed or changed during long-window extension, ablation, or backtesting.
22. Candidate screening must apply the four joint objectives in “Highest-Priority Objective.” Stronger candidates combine lower validation loss, more effective improvements, a completed-epoch count closer to `120`, and a best epoch closer to epoch `120`. Do not split the four objectives into a single-metric hierarchy, and do not substitute test loss, backtesting, or speed for the joint objective.
23. Before tuning, create a tuning-scope ledger that marks every relevant dimension as user-fixed, searched, or excluded with evidence and records its value, range, or exclusion reason. A user-fixed dimension remains unchanged in every trial, rerun, and ablation. Fixing one dimension must not freeze any unspecified dimension, and every fixed value must still be saved in each candidate's config snapshot.
24. Unless fixed by the user or excluded as inapplicable with evidence, the scope must consider at least model structure and capacity, the learning-rate range, optimizer and optimizer parameters, scheduler and scheduler parameters, sample characteristics such as sliding-window length, and the interactions among window length, batch size, and learning rate. Staged search must not become a reason to omit an unfixed dimension.
25. When label definitions, class counts, or loss definitions make `val_loss` incomparable, split trials into separate comparability groups. Trials from different groups must not enter one four-objective ranking. If the label itself is tunable, determine the label contract separately, then freeze it and select the final `5` candidates within one comparable group. Test loss, backtesting, and error distributions may inform the label decision but must not mix incomparable validation losses into one ranking.

3. Use `$cx-common-module` to check whether training entrypoints, backtest entrypoints, config hooks, recipes, resource monitoring, metric aggregation, and output persistence already have functional entrypoints. If not, define the calling model first.
4. Use `$cx-pytorch-tdd` to verify standalone training, standalone backtesting, config clone/override hooks, trial-to-config mapping, resource monitoring, metric parsing, and output persistence paths.
5. Record business meaning, label relationship, distribution, and leakage risk for every field actually used. Run contribution experiments and candidate comparisons only for tunable fields; fixed fields do not consume trial budget for repeated selection.
6. When data dimensions are tunable, use one fixed model to validate field combinations and label definitions, then produce data-recipe candidates. Skip each fixed dimension in search, but keep its semantic and leakage evidence. Keep different label or loss contracts in separate comparability groups.
7. When window length is tunable, search window length only from `3` through `20` days and search batch size with it. Run comparable trials for safe candidates and evaluate them with the four joint objectives; speed only constrains the experiment budget. When the window is fixed, preserve it and search the remaining unfixed batch and training dimensions.
8. Search the unfixed learning-rate range, weight decay, optimizer and optimizer parameters, and scheduler and scheduler parameters after the window/batch budget is known; when batch size changes, retune learning rate with it. For a learning-rate warmup and cooldown cycle, save the scheduler name, warmup epochs, cooldown epochs, cycle count, maximum learning rate, minimum learning rate, change function, and actual patience.
9. Search unfixed model-structure parameters such as hidden size, layers, dropout, encoder length, capacity, and complexity. Skip this stage only when the user fixed the model structure or an evidence-backed exclusion applies.
10. Rerun each stage's best recipe with the same seed config value `3407`, then record the best trial, candidates, each model's per-model best recipe, failed trials, pruned trials, five-minute tracking summaries, data version, field recommendations, and convergence evidence in the target `docs/cx` task, change, or experiment summary. Stage results only narrow ranges and must not permanently lock an early single-point winner.
11. After all unfixed dimensions have been searched, jointly rerun every retained full recipe and review interactions among window length, batch size, learning rate, optimizer, scheduler, and model structure before forming an initial set of exactly `5` candidates. Use the four joint highest-priority objectives. When the objectives trade off, preserve different tradeoffs and document the evidence for each. Test loss and business metrics only confirm generalization and business behavior; a single high backtest or faster run must not replace a candidate that is stronger on the joint objective.
12. When window length is tunable, hold every other parameter fixed for the strongest initial candidate and test `30`, `40`, `50`, and `60` days. Compare each long-window result with the original baseline on the four joint objectives; update that recipe only when the result is not dominated by the baseline and provides stronger joint evidence, otherwise keep the original window. Then jointly rerun all five final complete recipes and confirm the final five with the four joint objectives; skip extension when the window is fixed. Run ablation and backtesting for each final candidate. Every ablation uses the same one-tenth complete-entity sample, seed config value `3407`, `120`-epoch cap, and default early-stopping patience `20`; a learning-rate cycle keeps its recorded actual patience and full cycle parameters. User-fixed features and parameters remain unchanged. Save each candidate's baseline, ablation variants, and backtest result before handing all `5` candidates to full HPO.
13. When the user requests business-first reporting, show the five candidates with Top10 hit rate, missed-candidate mean gain, downside share, downside mean loss, and `val_loss`, while preserving all four joint-objective views. Business metrics may confirm candidates whose four objectives are comparable, but they must not replace those objectives in admission.

## Evaluation Criteria

- The four joint objectives share the highest priority: lower `best_val_loss`, more effective improvements, completed epochs closer to `120`, and a best epoch closer to epoch `120`. No single objective decides admission.
- Compare the four objectives only under the same label, validation set, loss definition, validation cadence, effective-improvement threshold, and training cap. A candidate that is no better on all four than another candidate must not rank ahead of it; preserve and explain tradeoffs without an unapproved weighting formula.
- Different labels, class counts, or loss definitions form separate comparability groups and must not enter one four-objective ranking. The final five candidates come from one already determined label and loss contract.
- Final validation loss, improvement ratio, and best-to-final-epoch distance remain diagnostic evidence, but do not replace the four objectives. In particular, a short distance after early stopping does not make the best epoch late.
- `test_loss` confirms whether validation improvement generalizes and must not replace the four objectives.
- Backtesting confirms business behavior and must not replace the four objectives. When the four objectives and `test_loss` are comparable, backtesting may explain candidate differences.
- When backtesting is strong but loss is clearly worse, first investigate chance variation, label misalignment, class boundaries, sample selection, and return-statistic definitions.
- Top10 business metrics are mandatory for stock-candidate reporting: higher hit rate is better, hit-candidate mean gain confirms hit quality, higher missed-candidate mean gain is better, lower downside share is better, and a smaller absolute downside mean loss is better. They only explain candidates and confirm business behavior when the four objectives are comparable; they neither affect training or early stopping nor replace the four objectives in admission.
